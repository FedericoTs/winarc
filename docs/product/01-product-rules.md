# Product rules

The twelve rules in `CLAUDE.md` are the contract. This page explains why each exists, so a future change is made knowingly.

## 1. Squad terms live on the squad

Stakes must be uniform inside a squad or the pot is unfair and the ledger is unreadable. The founder chooses size, stake tier and pot rule when creating the squad. Anyone who joins with the code inherits them, which also removes a whole screen from the invitee's onboarding.

Size is two to eight. Two is a pact, five to eight is a squad. A squad that falls under three active members, or under its size for a pact, dissolves and the survivors are re-drafted.

## 2. Stake the process, never the outcome

Money rides on sessions and habits, never on kilograms. The weigh-in is weekly, private and optional, and the squad sees an on-track badge, not a number, unless the member opts in. There are no calorie targets and no weight goals in absolute numbers anywhere. Under-18s cannot set weight tracking. This is the safety answer to the hustle-culture critique and it keeps the product on the right side of body-image harm.

## 3. A miss never resets the arc

The week-three quit is a shame spiral. A miss costs the stake and marks the day. Streaks count consecutive squad days, and a broken streak restarts, but the arc, the contract and the squad are untouched. Rescue mechanics are visible on the home screen: one sick day per fortnight, one vouch per week, an appeal. Showing the exit is what keeps people in.

## 4. The app asks, it never rejects

Below the confidence floor the member sees "We couldn't verify this one," never "rejected" or anything that implies cheating. The order is retake, then vouch, then appeal. Nothing is charged on an ask. A false reject on day three is a deleted app; a kind ask is a retake.

## 5. Tiers

Gold is Health evidence plus photo. Silver is photo only. Bronze is vouched by two squadmates. All three are hits on the board and the ledger. Tiers exist so the stamp can be honest about its evidence, not to rank members.

## 6. Dual-cam, in-app only

Rear then front, about a second apart, from the app's own camera. No gallery uploads. This is both the fraud posture and the share format: BeReal made the two-frame image iconic.

## 7. Season one money is a ledger

The pot is virtual. Misses become ledger entries the squad settles between themselves and spends by vote at the finale. No card, no charge, no payout, no money held, which means no money-transmission exposure and no chargebacks. Revenue is the Arc Pass through in-app purchase only. Charity and anti-charity beneficiaries arrive in season two with a legal entity and counsel.

## 8. No feed

Squad-only visibility. Nobody performs for strangers, and the app is not a worse Strava. The public surfaces are share cards and the web join page, both of which the member chooses to publish.

## 9. No coaching

Apple, Strava and Whoop commoditized AI coaching in 2026. We verify and narrate. Their progress does not touch us.

## 10. Strava is personal-only

Strava's API terms forbid showing a member's Strava data to anyone else and forbid AI use of the data. So Strava never appears on a squad surface and never feeds verification. HealthKit and Health Connect are the verification sources and already receive Garmin, Apple Watch, Withings, Oura and Whoop data.

## 11. One home squad per contract

Shared fate needs one ledger. What people want from "multiple squads" is multiple communities, which is a crew: a creator's audience, a gym, a city, holding many squads with squad-versus-squad tables. Crews come later.

## 12. The screenshot test

Every share card is 9:16, carries one number, the member's identity, the squad, a watermark and the join code, and reads at thumbnail size. Cards are the acquisition channel.
